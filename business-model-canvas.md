# Business Model Canvas

The Business Model Canvas visually describes how a company creates, delivers, and captures value. More actionable than lengthy business plans.

## Nine Blocks

### 1. Customer Segments
Who are we serving?

```
Examples:
- Startups (vs enterprises vs SMBs)
- Specific industries (healthcare, fintech, etc.)
- Specific geographies
- Price-sensitive vs quality-focused customers

Segmentation tips:
- Different needs: Product features matter differently
- Different channels: How to reach them differs
- Different relationships: Support level differs
- Different willingness to pay: Pricing differs
```

### 2. Value Propositions
What problems do we solve? What benefits do we offer?

```
Effectiveness:
- Do task faster
- Better quality
- Lower cost
- Reduce risk

Desirability:
- Status/prestige
- Aesthetics
- Environmental impact
- Social impact

Example:
- For small businesses: "Save $10K/year on payroll admin"
- For large enterprises: "99.99% uptime SLA with white-glove support"
```

### 3. Revenue Streams
How do we make money?

```
Models:
- Subscription (recurring: SaaS)
- Freemium (free with paid tier)
- Pay-per-use (consumption-based)
- Licensing (sell once, recurring fees)
- Transaction fees (take %, like Stripe)
- Advertising (free product, ads revenue)
- Marketplace (take % of transactions)

Example SaaS mix:
- 70% annual subscriptions
- 20% implementation/consulting
- 10% premium support
```

### 4. Key Resources
What assets do we need to deliver value?

```
Types:
- Physical (servers, stores, vehicles)
- Intellectual (IP, patents, brand)
- Human (team expertise)
- Financial (capital, credit)

For SaaS:
- Servers/infrastructure
- Engineering team
- Product IP (code, algorithms)
- Brand trust
```

### 5. Key Activities
What must we do to operate?

```
Examples:
- Product development
- Customer acquisition
- Customer support
- Content marketing
- Partnerships

Priority order (80/20):
- Which 3-5 activities drive 80% of success?
- Focus there, outsource the rest
```

### 6. Key Partnerships
Who do we collaborate with?

```
Types:
- Suppliers (provide inputs)
- Complementors (add value)
- Co-production partners (make product together)
- Platform partners (reach customers)

Examples:
- SaaS needs: Cloud provider, payment processor, API partners
- Marketplace: Sellers (suppliers), buyers (customers), payment processor
```

### 7. Customer Relationships
How do we interact with customers?

```
Types:
- Self-service (minimal interaction)
- Automated service (email, self-help)
- Personal assistance (dedicated support)
- Dedicated account manager (enterprise)
- Community (user forums, peer support)

Cost consideration:
- Self-service: Low cost, low touch, high volume
- Dedicated support: High cost, high touch, small segment
```

### 8. Channels
How do we reach customers?

```
Awareness (top of funnel):
- Content marketing
- Social media
- Ads
- PR
- Partnerships

Acquisition:
- Sales team
- Self-serve signup
- Marketplace
- Partnerships
- Affiliate

Support:
- Help center
- Email support
- Chat/phone
- Community forum
```

### 9. Cost Structure
What does it cost to operate?

```
Fixed costs (constant):
- Salaries
- Rent
- Infrastructure (cloud)

Variable costs (per customer):
- Payment processor fees
- Customer support
- Content delivery
- Marketing spend

Example SaaS breakdown:
- 40% engineering/product
- 25% sales/marketing
- 20% ops/infrastructure
- 15% G&A
```

## Canvas Exercise

### Fill-In Exercise (60 minutes)
```
1. Customer Segments (10 min)
   - Who are your top 3 customer segments?
   - Why are they different?

2. Value Propositions (15 min)
   - What 3 problems do you solve per segment?
   - How are you better than competitors?

3. Channels (10 min)
   - How do customers discover you?
   - How do they purchase?
   - How do they get support?

4. Customer Relationships (5 min)
   - How much hand-holding per segment?
   - Cost of support?

5. Revenue Streams (10 min)
   - How much willingly pay per segment?
   - What revenue model per segment?

6. Key Resources (5 min)
   - What 3-5 critical resources?
   - Owned vs contracted?

7. Key Activities (5 min)
   - What 3-5 things must you do well?

8. Key Partnerships (5 min)
   - Who are critical partners?
   - Why partner vs build?

9. Cost Structure (5 min)
   - Fixed vs variable costs?
   - Cost per customer?

Result: Full Business Model Canvas
```

## Assumption Testing

### Riskiest Assumptions (Lean Canvas approach)
```
Every business has assumptions:

Assumption 1: Customers have this problem
   Test: Interview 10 potential customers
   How: "Do you struggle with X?"
   Pass: 7+ confirm problem exists

Assumption 2: Customers will pay for solution
   Test: Landing page + payment intent
   How: "Would you pay $X/month?" (collect emails from interested)
   Pass: 30%+ indicate interest

Assumption 3: We can reach customers cost-effectively
   Test: Pilot marketing campaign
   How: $1K budget on top channel
   Pass: CAC < $500

Assumption 4: Product works as intended
   Test: MVP with beta users
   How: 10 early users for 1 month
   Pass: 80%+ would recommend

Assumption 5: Unit economics work
   Test: Cohort analysis after 3 months
   How: LTV:CAC ratio
   Pass: > 3:1
```

### Testing Roadmap
```
Week 1-2: Problem validation
- Talk to 10-20 potential customers
- Confirm problem is severe enough to pay for solution

Week 3-4: Solution validation
- Show mockup/demo
- Get commitment (would you pay $X?)

Week 5-8: MVP/Beta
- Build minimal viable product
- Get 5-10 beta users
- Measure: retention, NPS, usage

Week 9-12: Pilot launch
- Marketing campaign ($1-5K budget)
- Measure: CAC, conversion, retention

Week 13+: Scale if metrics work
- If LTV:CAC > 3:1, scale
- If not, pivot
```

## Pivots

When assumptions fail, pivot:

```
Pivot 1: Pivot the problem
Problem: Nobody wants CRM for photographers
Solution: Find different use case for same tool (freelancers? agencies?)

Pivot 2: Pivot the market segment
Current: Small businesses don't have money
Solution: Target enterprises, non-profits, schools instead

Pivot 3: Pivot the revenue model
Current: Subscription doesn't work (low LTV)
Solution: Try transaction fees (take % of sales)

Pivot 4: Pivot the feature
Current: All-in-one CRM nobody uses completely
Solution: Best-in-class email integration only

Pivot 5: Go up-market or down-market
Current: Targeting SMBs (crowded)
Solution: Go enterprise (less competition, higher ACV)

Document pivot decision:
- What assumption failed?
- What new assumption are we testing?
- Expected timeline to decision?
```

## Version Control

```
Version 1.0 (Launch): Initial hypothesis
- Assumptions: [list 5-10]
- Risk level: Very high (all untested)

Version 1.1 (After problem validation)
- Confirmed: Customers have problem
- Adjusted: Target market more specific
- New risk: Solution doesn't exist / expensive

Version 2.0 (After MVP validation)
- Confirmed: Customers like MVP, 80% retention
- Pivot: Revenue model from subscription to freemium
- New risk: Freemium conversion rate unknown

Version 2.1 (After growth metrics)
- Confirmed: LTV:CAC 2.5:1 (good enough)
- Staying course: Double down on current market
- Risk: Need better retention to improve unit economics
```

## Deployment Checklist

- [ ] Business Model Canvas filled out (all 9 blocks)
- [ ] Customer segments clearly defined
- [ ] Value propositions tested with customers
- [ ] Revenue model defined and validated
- [ ] Unit economics calculated (LTV, CAC, payback)
- [ ] Riskiest assumptions identified
- [ ] Assumption testing plan created
- [ ] MVP scope defined
- [ ] Go/no-go criteria set for each test
- [ ] Pivot decision criteria documented
- [ ] Canvas updated quarterly or on major pivot
- [ ] Team aligned on current model
